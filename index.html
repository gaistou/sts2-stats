Methodology — Card effectiveness

A naive "winrate by card" stat is misleading: a card can only be picked up on the floor it's offered, so a card typically offered late in Act 1 can only ever belong to a run that had already survived almost the whole act. That run was already very likely to clear Act 1 regardless of the card — so late-offered cards look artificially great, no matter how good they actually are.

To avoid that, Act 1 card effectiveness here only counts a card if it was picked up by floor 8 (roughly the midpoint of Act 1), and only compares it against other runs that had also already reached floor 8 — whether they picked the card up or not. So "having the card" isn't just a stand-in for "survived long enough to get it": every run being compared already cleared the same amount of ground. The tradeoff: cards that are typically offered later in Act 1 are underrepresented, since those late picks simply aren't counted.

How to read the table: N is how many times the card was picked up by floor 8. With is the Act 1 clear rate (reached Act 2) among runs that had the card by floor 8; Without is the same clear rate for runs that didn't. Diff is With minus Without. Be careful with rows that have a very low N (rarely picked) — their numbers can swing wildly and are easy to misread as a strong signal when it's really just a small sample.

The separate Global winrate (all 3 acts) column is a different question entirely, and deliberately not floor-restricted: it's the full-run win rate (won the whole game, not just Act 1) among any run that had the card at any point, picked up on any floor of any act. Because of that, it has the exact same late-pickup survivorship bias described above — a card that's mostly obtained near the end of a run will look great here even if it's mediocre. Read it as "of the runs that ended up with this card, how many won," not as evidence the card is strong early.

The main With / Without / Diff columns pool every ascension level, for more volume, at the cost of mixing difficulty levels where a card's power can differ. The Δ (A9/A10 only) column re-runs the same floor-8 comparison restricted to ascension 9-10 runs only, as a sanity check on whether the main result still holds at high ascension; it respects the same min-sample filter as the main columns, applied to that restricted sample specifically — a dash means no A9/A10 data existed at all, an empty cell means some existed but not enough to clear the current min-sample threshold.

Each card is also only scored using runs played after its most recent balance patch (see last changed patch column) — a buffed or nerfed card isn't evaluated using data from its old, different version.

This is correlational, not causal: card picks are the player's choice, not a random draw, so a stronger player who also happens to pick well will show up on both sides. Treat the numbers as a signal worth investigating, not proof a card is good or bad.

Methodology — Smith value

A different question from card effectiveness above: given a card is already in the deck, is it worth Smith-upgrading at a campfire? The naive comparison (runs that ended with an upgraded copy vs runs that didn't) is badly biased — you can only end up with an upgraded copy after surviving long enough to reach a campfire in the first place, so upgraded copies look artificially good regardless of whether the upgrade itself helped.

The fix: campfires cluster on a handful of fixed floor ranges rather than being spread evenly through Act 1. For every run that still owns an unupgraded copy of a card at a campfire, we record whether it Smithed that card right there or did something else (Heal, Smith a different card) — and which floor range that campfire falls in. Both sides of the comparison are, by construction, runs that already reached that same floor range, so the "survived longer" confound is gone. Only a run's first such opportunity per card counts, and results are combined across floor ranges with a stratified average so a floor range with a naturally higher baseline clear rate (e.g. one right before the Act 1 boss) doesn't skew the overall number.

How to read the table: n (smithed) / n (kept base) are the two group sizes. Smith % / Base % are the subsequent Act 2 clear rate for each group; Diff is Smith minus Base. Unlike the card-effectiveness report above, this one pools all ascension levels rather than just A9/A10 — Act 1 campfire choices are rarer events than card pickups, and the extra volume noticeably stabilizes the numbers, at the cost of mixing difficulty levels where a card's power can differ. The Δ (A9/A10 only) column re-runs the same method restricted to ascension 9-10 runs only, as a sanity check on whether the main columns still hold at high ascension; it respects the same min-sample filter as the main columns, applied to that restricted sample specifically — a dash means no A9/A10 data existed at all, an empty cell means some existed but not enough to clear the current min-sample threshold.

Same caveats as above: correlational, not causal, and each card is only scored against runs on a patch at or after its most recent balance change. Starting-deck cards (Strike, Defend, and each character's two unique starting cards) are included by treating them as owned from floor 1, rather than requiring a reward pickup like every other card.

Methodology — Rare card (boss)

The Act 1 boss always offers a choice of 3 rare cards as its reward. Does which one you keep correlate with winning the whole run? Unlike the Act 1 card effectiveness report above, no landmark-floor bias correction is needed here: every run being compared already beat the Act 1 boss, so every run faced the exact same choice at the exact same point — there's no "survived longer, so naturally looks better" confound to control for.

Cohort: runs that beat the Act 1 boss (won the game, or kept going past that floor). Runs that died to the boss itself never got the choice and aren't counted.

How to read the table: n is how many runs kept that card. Winrate is the full-run (3 acts) win rate for those runs. Diff vs baseline is that winrate minus the baseline winrate for this character's cohort. (skipped) covers runs that beat the boss but kept none of the 3 rares offered — a real comparison point, not noise. Rows marked * have a sample below the current minimum-sample filter (40 by default, adjustable).

The main columns pool every ascension level, for more volume, at the cost of mixing difficulty levels where a card's value can differ. The Δ (A9/A10 only) column re-runs the same comparison restricted to ascension 9-10 runs only, as a sanity check on whether the main result still holds at high ascension; it respects the same min-sample filter as the main column, applied to that restricted sample specifically — a dash means no A9/A10 data existed at all, an empty cell means some existed but not enough to clear the current min-sample threshold.

Each card is only scored using runs played after its most recent balance patch, same as the Act 1 card effectiveness report. Same caveat too: correlational, not causal — which rare card a player keeps is a choice, not a coin flip, so a stronger player's picks will still tend to win more regardless of the card itself.

Neow starting relic winrate (all ascensions, all characters)

  • Neow relics
Methodology

Simpler than the two reports above: does starting a run with relic X from Neow correlate with winning the whole game? Neow's choice is always made on floor 1, and every run reaches it, so there's no landmark-floor bias to correct for here — comparing "took relic X" against "didn't" is a valid straight comparison, unlike the floor-8/floor-range restrictions the other two reports need.

How to read the table: n is how many runs started with that relic. Winrate is the full-run win rate for those runs. Diff vs baseline is that winrate minus the overall baseline winrate across every run in the character filter. Runs where Neow granted no relic at all (the player picked gold, max HP, or a card removal instead) are bucketed as (no relic) — a real, informative comparison point, not noise to discard.

The main columns pool every ascension level, for more volume, at the cost of mixing difficulty levels where a relic's value can differ. The Δ (A9/A10 only) column re-runs the same comparison restricted to ascension 9-10 runs only, as a sanity check on whether the main result still holds at high ascension; it respects the same min-sample filter as the main column, applied to that restricted sample specifically — a dash means no A9/A10 data existed at all, an empty cell means some existed but not enough to clear the current min-sample threshold.

Each relic is also only scored using runs played after its most recent balance patch (see last changed patch column) — a buffed, nerfed, or reworked relic isn't evaluated using data from its old, different version. Runs older than that are simply dropped for that relic (there's no other bucket to move them to, since a run's Neow pick is fixed). Most relics have never been changed and use the full dataset.

Use the character filter at the top of the table: relic strength varies a lot by class, so a relic's value is only meaningful within one character at a time, not pooled across all five.

Same caveat as everywhere else on this site: correlational, not causal. Neow's relic choice is partly the player's pick and partly luck of what's offered, and a stronger player's runs will still tend to win more regardless of relic.

Miscellaneous hypotheses

  • Miscellaneous hypotheses
Methodology

Small, one-off questions that don't warrant their own dedicated report. Unlike every other report on this site, each hypothesis here is scored as a single number across all 5 characters combined, not broken out per character. The page opens with a "Fast facts" box — one line per hypothesis — before the full breakdown for each.

Where a hypothesis involves a "did X happen early" question (e.g. visiting a shop by a certain floor), the same landmark-floor logic as the Act 1 card effectiveness report applies: the cohort is restricted to runs that reached at least that floor, so neither comparison group is favored just for having survived longer.

Combining across characters uses a stratified average (the diff is computed separately per character, then averaged weighted by sample size) rather than pooling every run together directly — the same technique the Act 1 card upgrade value report uses to combine across floor ranges, applied here to combine across characters instead. This avoids a character with both a different baseline clear rate and a different rate of doing the thing in question from skewing the combined number. The displayed "with" / "without" percentages are the plain pooled rates across all characters, for readability — the diff is the number that's actually bias-corrected.

Same caveat as everywhere else on this site: correlational, not causal.
